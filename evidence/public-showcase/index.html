01 · The problem

A protein model can look convincing and still support the wrong conclusion.

Residue numbering can drift. Predicted confidence can be mistaken for experimental validation. A conformation can be labeled “active” without two-sided references. A surface can be called exposed without biological context.

YAUVI turns those hidden assumptions into visible inputs, measurements, limitations, and reproducible files.

02 · Six scientific questions

Start with the question—not a black-box score.

Each workflow keeps its evidence dimension separate and carries an explicit claim ceiling.

04 · Executed demonstrations

Six traceable evidence cases.

Five use invented scientific inputs. SF-CSA tests the canonical pipeline with disclosed alignment-engine test doubles. These are software demonstrations—not biological or patient findings.

05 · Independent public qualification

What agreed—and what did not.

These cases use checksum-locked public records and real scientific runtimes. A passed public case is stronger than a synthetic demonstration, but it does not establish accuracy across every protein class.

Evidence identity

Reproduce the exact decision trail.

06 · Who benefits

One evidence trail, read at different depths.

Researchers

Catch structural assumptions before they shape experiments.

Review mappings, references, geometry, interfaces, and missing evidence in one traceable workflow.

Students

Learn what each computational result can and cannot mean.

Plain-language explanations sit beside methods, units, uncertainty, and raw tables.

Collaborators

Share the same exact model, question, and evidence boundary.

Checksum-bound reports reduce ambiguity across computational and experimental teams.

Reviewers

Inspect methods and raw outputs instead of trusting a screenshot.

Deterministic evidence bundles expose inputs, parameters, versions, and limitations.
